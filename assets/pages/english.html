Su Dongpo, read slowly

Su Shi (苏轼, 1037–1101), art name Dongpo, is the writer Chinese readers return to when fortune turns. This site is a public guide: his life as a map of exile, the poems in verified text, and the people, meals, calligraphy, and folklore that grew up around him.

Who he was

Born in Meishan, Sichuan, Su Shi passed the palace examination at twenty-one and spent the rest of his life between high office and exile. He opposed Wang Anshi’s New Policies, survived the Crow Terrace poetry trial (乌台诗案) in 1079, and wrote the work he is loved for while banished — to Huangzhou, then Huizhou, then Danzhou on Hainan. He died in Changzhou in 1101, on the way back north. The Southern Song court later gave him the posthumous name Wenzhong (文忠).

Ask what I achieved in this life: Huangzhou, Huizhou, Danzhou.— Su Shi, “Self-inscription on the Jinshan portrait” (1101). Translation by this site.

The translations on this page are ours, made for orientation. They are not a substitute for the Chinese, which remains the text.

Four lines to start with

The great river runs east; its waves have washed away the heroes of a thousand years.大江东去，浪淘尽，千古风流人物。 — “Niannujiao · Recalling the Red Cliff”
A straw cape in the mist and rain — let this life be as it is.一蓑烟雨任平生。 — “Dingfengbo”
May we live long, and share this bright moon across a thousand miles.但愿人长久，千里共婵娟。 — “Shuidiao getou”
I cannot see the true face of Mount Lu, because I am standing on the mountain.不识庐山真面目，只缘身在此山中。 — “Written on the Wall of West Forest Temple”

How the site is organized

The reading pages are in Chinese. Each one is a single subject, with sources named in the text.

  • Life — a chronology from Meishan to Danzhou, including the Crow Terrace case.
  • Works — checked original texts with notes written for this site.
  • Masters on Dongpo — later writers, each quotation sourced.
  • People — Su Zhe, Ouyang Xiu, Wang Anshi, Zhang Dun, Foyin, Wang Fu, Zhaoyun.
  • A lived life — food, jokes, and the letters from exile.
  • Calligraphy and painting — the Cold Food scroll and where the surviving works are kept.
  • Folklore — stories marked as history, notebook anecdote, or legend.
  • Lines — short quotations grouped by theme, each with a source.
  • Exam writing — a method for using Su Shi in Chinese-language essays.
  • A life to measure by — youth, middle age, old age.
  • Search — titles, lines, and pinyin.
